# MIDNIGHT PINBALL

A deterministic three-ball pinball table with a two-minute ranked ceiling. Hold and release LAUNCH to plunge. PRIMARY and SECONDARY operate independent left and right flippers. Directional input nudges the cabinet; repeated nudges accumulate tilt and disable scoring, flippers, and ball save until the next regular ball.

Hit the three gold star targets at the back of the table, then shoot the left SKYLINE scoop upward to unlock three-ball multiball. The first bank awards a bonus; the completed lit ramp raises the multiplier and releases two physical extra balls. Ramp completions in multiball award 5,000 × multiplier jackpots. Bumpers award 100, targets 500, slings 30, ordinary ramps 1,500. A target hit during the first three seconds of a launch awards a 2,000-point skill shot. An eight-second launch save and ten-second multiball save return drained balls. Losing all active balls outside save consumes one of three regular balls. A new simulation fully resets the run.

The model runs at 60 Hz with six fixed physics substeps. Balls use swept circle-versus-circle and circle-versus-capsule contacts, including rail endpoints. The table incline accelerates balls toward the drain. Motorized capsule flippers have separate angular positions; their contact velocity is derived from angular velocity and the actual contact point, so timing and ball position change shot speed and direction. Held flippers stop imparting motor energy. Slings and pop bumpers kick on contact with short, bounded cooldowns. Multiball includes equal-mass ball contacts.

This is a custom bounded arcade physics simulation, not a general rigid-body engine. The elevated ramp uses a constrained three-dimensional centerline with simulated distance, speed, gravitational potential loss, and downhill acceleration. An underpowered shot rolls back. A successful shot visibly rises over two table units above the playfield, bypasses mechanisms below, and drops under vertical gravity after leaving the exit. The wireform floor/rails and supports are actual Three.js meshes matching that path. It does not simulate free lateral rolling within the wireform or unconstrained ball spin. The table, cabinet, flippers, bumpers, sling plastics, rails, targets and balls are procedural 3D geometry; lettering is generated locally on textures. No downloaded assets or physics dependencies are used.

The seeded simulation has no DOM access, wall clock or unseeded randomness, and cannot advance past 7,200 ticks. Collision iteration counts, active balls (three), visual sparks (thirty), meshes and temporary feedback are bounded. The orthographic camera fits the whole physical cabinet across portrait and landscape viewports. Reduced motion disables sparks; high contrast raises rail contrast and outlines balls. All view geometries, materials, label textures and scene objects are disposed together.

Regression coverage exercises charged launch and shooter-lane exit, swept rails, independently firing flippers, bumper/target contacts, elevated and stalled ramps, earned multiball and jackpot, altitude separation, ball collisions, drain/save/three-ball termination, fresh reset, tilt/cooldown, the exact time cap, and a 7,200-frame seeded replay.

Replay optimization caches static capsule normalization and unchanged flipper geometry, rejects unreachable mechanisms with conservative swept bounds, and removes transient per-mechanism metadata objects. All surviving contacts retain the same sweep equations, order, six substeps and five contact iterations. An offline comparison checked every state and signal for 7,200 frames under five input/scenario patterns and three seeds, including continuous two- and three-ball stress, against the preceding implementation; every frame matched exactly.

On October 25, 2026, a local Node wall-time benchmark used three warm-up runs and nine measured runs per pattern across seeds 1, 1980 and 0x12345678. Maximum times before → after were: neutral 1.38 → 1.35 ms; backend active-input pattern 85.49 → 10.53 ms; reactive flippers 115.44 → 12.66 ms; continuous two-ball stress 162.58 → 21.62 ms; continuous three-ball stress 231.60 → 27.68 ms. Each replay ran all 7,200 calls; stress fixtures kept their respective ball counts active throughout using a fixed save window. These local timings describe these fixtures and hardware, and do not establish production Worker CPU usage or guarantee any provider's free-tier quota.
